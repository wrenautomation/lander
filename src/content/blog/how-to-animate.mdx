This file stays a draft. Name a post `.mdx` instead of `.md` to use the blocks below. No import line needed.

<Steps>

1. The steps come in one at a time as the reader scrolls.
2. Write them as a normal numbered list inside the block.
3. In the email they read as the same list.

</Steps>

<Note title="Keep it short">
One thing a skimmer must not miss. In the email the title becomes the first line.
</Note>

<Compare before="By hand" after="With Wren">

- Copy the lead into the CRM
- Write the follow-up

---

- The lead lands in the CRM
- The follow-up is drafted for you

</Compare>

Leave a blank line inside `Steps` and `Compare` so the list inside reads as Markdown. In `Compare`, a `---` line splits the before list from the after list.

## Figures that build as you scroll

Each one wraps a short list. On the page the list is drawn and builds one line at a time; in the email it is the title, then the list. Keep items short: one line on a phone.

<Rack title="A rack, one unit per line" fig="Fig. 1">

- **First**: slides in and lights up
- **Second**: then this one
- **Third**: and so on, up to eight

</Rack>

<Leaks title="Leaks, one joint per line" fig="Fig. 2">

- **First**: a collar seals this joint
- **Second**: and the tank fills a notch

</Leaks>

<Fan title="One source, many outputs" from="One idea" fig="Fig. 3">

- **First**: a wire to each line
- **Second**: up to eight

</Fan>

<Timeline title="Stops in order" fig="Fig. 4">

- **Day 1**: the first stop
- **Day 3**: the next
- **Day 7**: the last

</Timeline>

<Stat n="23" suffix="%">
is how a Stat reads: the number, then the sentence that finishes it, with its source linked.
</Stat>

`Rack` and `Leaks` take `unit` for the counter at the bottom ("running", "sealed"). `Stat` takes `prefix`, `n` and `suffix`; only use a number you can source. Only one figure animates at a time, and readers who ask for less motion see each one finished.
