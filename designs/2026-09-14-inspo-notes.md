## Patterns across all fifteen

- Every CTA names something free: free call, free analysis, free
  diagnostic, free scorecard. Ours says "Request an audit". The word
  free is missing.
- Numbers first, story second. The best case titles are the number.
- Testimonials that work carry a number and a full name, title, company.
- The good ones hand you the objection before you raise it: guarantee,
  no contract, you own it.
- Two devices that make the reader act: pick a side (nuage) and prove
  it (usman).
- Design that stands out is one of: numbered sections and whitespace
  (havu), or a single interactive thing (usman). Nobody wins on template.

## Take now

1. **Free in the CTA.** "Request an audit" → "Get the free audit". Hero,
   nav, form button. The email already says free.
2. **Number as the case title.** Kind line becomes the result:
   "6 to 7 hours a week back for 17,000 staff" over Government of
   Canada; "200 hours of lookups, gone" over University of Alberta.
   Client name and year under it.
3. **Prove it links.** Each pillar and each "what this proves" point
   links to the case that proves it. One word link, "see it".
4. **Pick a side.** One short block above the targets list: two columns,
   "by hand" vs "runs itself", four rows each, in the niche's words. The
   reader finds their column.
5. **Them vs us table** (havu), our version: "a typical automation
   agency" vs "me", five rows: who does the work, what you get at the
   end, what happens if it breaks, price, lock-in. Facts we already say
   in the pillars, in a shape that reads in 10 seconds.
6. **Name the audit output.** growmyads names what the free thing
   finds. Ours: "a one page list of what to automate first, what it
   saves, and what it costs. Yours to keep either way."
7. **One education line** in the pain section (outpace): why the same
   figure ends up in five places, and why nobody fixed it. Then the
   turn.
8. **Numbered sections** and more whitespace (havu). Cheap, and it
   matches our hairline motif.
9. **FAQ stays**, but reorder to the questions people ask before a
   call, havu style: can I bring you in before I know what I want, do
   you work with our IT person, what if it breaks.

  studies carry it.
- **Photo of a person.** direction.com's cards work because of faces.

  After the first RIA build.
- **Aggregate proof** (reviews, retention, years). Needs clients.
- **Guarantee in contracts** (axe). We say "fix at no charge". A written
  guarantee needs a contract template first.
- **Free downloadable guide** (direction, bop). Later; a one pager "the
  five documents that repeat the same figure" could be the RIA one.
- **Wider offer** (thrive's ecosystem). Only after the document work
  converts.

## Copy from their testimonials anyway

No quotes yet, but the shape transfers:

- Put the number inside a sentence, the way a client would say it:
  "The people who did the most looking up get 6 to 7 hours a week back."
  Already close. Keep it in that voice on the case card.
- KPI row under each case, three plain labels (direction): hours a
  week, people, documents. We have the figures; add the labels.
- Name the role who benefits, since we can't name the person:
  "the policy staff", "the researchers who give the talks".
- "Still in use today" is our tenure stat. Say it once per case, the way
  thrive says 95% retention.
- When a quote arrives, format is quote, full name, title, company,
  number under it. Build the slot now, hide it until filled.
